One research theme, three complementary studies

Learning to generate signals for fetal monitoring research.

Cardiotocography (CTG) records fetal heart rate (FHR) alongside uterine contractions (UC). Limited data and imbalanced labels motivate research into synthetic signals that preserve useful temporal patterns.

This series explores three forms of conditioning: category labels in CTGGAN, physiological features in FHRDiff, and UC-informed FHR generation in CTGDiff. The first two studies generate FHR signals; CTGDiff extends the scope to UC and FHR synthesis.

Research directions

  • Category-level guidance

    Class-conditioned GANs

    Combine class labels, self-attention, residual learning, and adversarial training to generate FHR signals.

    CTGGAN ↗

  • Signal-specific guidance

    PRSA-conditioned diffusion

    Use physiological features represented as spectrograms to guide one-dimensional FHR denoising.

    FHRDiff ↗

  • Information across channels

    UC-informed FHR generation

    Generate a UC trace and use it alongside PRSA features to condition the synthesis of FHR.

    CTGDiff ↗

Publications

Three studies from IJCNN 2024, BIBM 2024, and ICASSP 2025. Each page includes the original model figure, results, available presentation materials, and citation.

CTGGAN

IJCNN 2024

Class-conditioned adversarial generation

CTGGAN: Reliable Fetal Heart Rate Signal Generation Using GANs

Zichang Yu, Yating Hu, Yu Lu, Leya Li, Huilin Ge, Xianghua Fu

Generate fetal heart rate signals from noise and a class label. Residual blocks, self-attention, and a Wasserstein objective with gradient penalty support the synthesis of temporal patterns.

FHR generation · Class labels · Residual and attention modules

FHRDiff

BIBM 2024

Physiological feature-conditioned diffusion

FHRDiff: Leveraging Diffusion Models for Conditional Fetal Heart Rate Signal Generation

Xiaoqing Li, Yu Lu, Kee Yuan Ngiam, Zichang Yu, Mohammad Shaheryar Furqan

Guide one-dimensional denoising with a spectrogram derived from phase-rectified signal averaging (PRSA). The paired condition provides signal-specific information for FHR generation.

FHR generation · PRSA spectrograms · Conditional diffusion

Project page ↗Paper PDFVideo MP4BibTeX

CTGDiff

ICASSP 2025

UC and FHR synthesis with cross-signal conditioning

CTGDiff: A Conditional Diffusion Model for Cardiotocography Signal Synthesis

Xiaoqing Li, Pufan Cai, Yu Lu, Shijie Shi, Liangkun Ma, Xianghua Fu

Generate uterine contraction signals first, then condition FHR generation on the synthesized UC trace and a PRSA spectrogram. This extends the output from FHR alone to both CTG channels.

UC generation · FHR generation · Sequential conditioning

Study scope

All three studies use CTU-UHB data, while their generated outputs, conditioning inputs, and evaluation settings differ.

Outputs, conditions, and evaluation focus
StudyGenerated signalsConditioning inputsEvaluation focus
CTGGANFHRCategory labelSignal fidelity and distribution similarity; selected 200-example dataset
FHRDiffFHRFHR PRSA spectrogramSignal quality, conditioning and diversity; expert review
CTGDiffUC, then FHRUC spectrogram; FHR PRSA spectrogram and UC traceFHR metrics, CTG examples, expert review, and real/synthetic training ratios

PRSA: phase-rectified signal averaging. The individual pages retain the results as reported in each paper. Differences in experimental settings mean the numbers should be interpreted within each study.
