One research theme, three complementary studies

Connecting cardiac ultrasound images with measurements of the left ventricle.

Left ventricular (LV) analysis in echocardiography depends on accurate cavity boundaries, anatomical landmarks, and consistent measurements over the cardiac cycle. Sparse annotations, ambiguous boundaries, and variation between frames make these tasks challenging.

This research series brings together three approaches: self-supervised multi-task learning in SSP-MAEFNet, anatomical semantic guidance in LV-SAM++, and spatiotemporal modeling in EchoST-SAM. Each study has its own task setting, experiments, and publication page.

Research directions

  • Learning from limited labels

    Annotation efficiency

    Use reconstruction pretraining and shared representations to learn LV segmentation and landmark detection from sparsely annotated videos.

    SSP-MAEFNet ↗

  • Bringing anatomy into SAM

    Anatomical guidance

    Combine medical semantic prompts, global image context, and local boundary detail to adapt a foundation model to cardiac ultrasound.

    LV-SAM++ ↗

  • Using the cardiac sequence

    Temporal consistency

    Model consecutive frames to improve the consistency of LV masks and the geometric readouts derived across a cardiac cycle.

    EchoST-SAM ↗

SSP-MAEFNet

ICME 2025

Self-supervised multi-task learning

Multi-Task Self-Supervised Learning for Automated Measurement of Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction in Echocardiography

Zhanpeng Xu, Yu Lu, Wei Zhang, Xiaoqing Li, Shijie Shi, Xianghua Fu

Learn from sparsely annotated echocardiographic videos. Masked reconstruction pretraining and a lightweight dual-task network jointly predict the LV cavity and anatomical landmarks, supporting volume and ejection fraction estimation.

LV segmentation · Apex and mitral-annulus landmarks · LVEF estimation

LV-SAM++

ICASSP 2026

Anatomical semantic guidance

LLM-Guided SAM for Left Ventricle Segmentation and Functional Analysis in Echocardiography

Xiaokang Deng, Yu Lu, Wei Zhang, Xianghua Fu

Adapt SAM to cardiac ultrasound with anatomical semantic prompts, automatic box prompts, and local feature enhancement. The predicted LV masks support analysis of cavity shape and area change.

LV segmentation · Eccentricity index (EI) · Area change ratio (ACR)

Project page ↗Paper PDFPoster PDFBibTeX

EchoST-SAM

IVCNZ 2026

Spatiotemporal modeling

EchoST-SAM: Semantic-Prompted Spatiotemporal LV Segmentation for Functional Analysis in Echocardiography

Yu Lu, Xiaokang Deng, Wei Zhang, Bingding Huang

Combine anatomical semantic guidance with five-frame temporal propagation. ConvLSTM modeling encourages consistency across consecutive LV masks and supports more stable geometric measurements over the cardiac cycle.

LV video segmentation · Temporal consistency · EI and ACR analysis

Evaluation scope

The studies cover related questions under different data and evaluation protocols.

Datasets, tasks, and reported measurements
StudyData settingTasks & readouts
SSP-MAEFNetEchoNet-Dynamic · apical four-chamber videosLV segmentation, anatomical landmarks, LV volumes, and ejection fraction (LVEF)
LV-SAM++CAMUS · apical two-chamber imagesLV segmentation, eccentricity index (EI), and area change ratio (ACR)
EchoST-SAMCAMUS · apical two-chamber sequences; study-specific patient split and intermediate-frame annotationsLV segmentation, temporal consistency, EI, and ACR; functional evaluation uses reference ED/ES frames

ED: end-diastole. ES: end-systole. EI describes cavity shape; ACR measures relative change in two-dimensional cavity area. EI and ACR are geometric readouts and should be distinguished from volumetric LVEF. Results should be interpreted within each paper’s evaluation protocol.
